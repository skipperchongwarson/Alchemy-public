## About

Teams rarely get stuck because they lack customer context or market data. And right now, it’s usually the opposite. They’re absolutely swimming in context: customer calls, research, analytics, support tickets, stakeholder opinions, a roadmap full of ideas, and whatever the competition's doing. The harder question to answer is, So what?! Which customer matters most? Which problem has enough teeth? What should change in the product or service? What do we stop doing? What actually gets built? Lots of questions. I’m a product strategist, service designer, and facilitator who works in that mess. With over 15+ years, I’ve worked across founding-stage startups, product studios, enterprise consulting, service design, and design leadership. I work in the gap between strategy and the details that make it real: shaping an opportunity or product direction, then dropping into interviews, workflows, service maps, prototypes, facilitation, critique, or the next decisions product and engineering teams have to make. Artificial intelligence (AI) is now part of that work, whether we agree with it or not, but my point of view is pretty simple: human judgment first, boosted by AI. No thank you to AI slop, as much as possible. I use AI to double check my work interrogating research and evidence, explore more directions, prototype faster, write and code, and turn information into something a team can actually use. The productivity gains can be real. But so can the verification cost and doing work multiple times over. I don’t think it's particularly goal to replace the person doing the work. It’s giving a skilled person more leverage while keeping context, judgment, accountability, and actual human evidence in the system. Take Replay, for example, I built a Claude-based evidence archive around 34 structured customer interviews so the team could query and revisit what people had actually said. AI didn’t replace the customer conversations. It made the human evidence more usable. I run How This Works co, where I help early-stage teams pressure-test the customer story behind consequential product and go-to-market decisions. The work ranges from focused customer discovery and ongoing listening cycles to product strategy, service design, facilitation, and fractional leadership. Across all of it, the underlying job is similar: turn messy evidence into a decision teams can act on. howthisworks.co/start … more
