## About

I tend to show up when a team has plenty of information and pressure to act—but still can't answer the question underneath it: So what?!

Which customer should matter most? What do we stop doing? What do we start? What does the evidence actually mean for the product?

I've spent 15+ years helping teams make those calls across product design, service design, strategy, customer research, facilitation, and design leadership.

I’ve been the first designer at a scrappy healthcare startup, a fractional founding product/design leader, a studio design director, and a regional design director. Along the way, I built a distributed 12-person design team at SoftServe, helped run thoughtbot’s San Francisco studio, led an 18-month strategy and design program for Duke Energy, and worked with startups like Shep and Sesame through launch, repositioning, and major product shifts.

These days, I'm running How This Works co. The practice grew out of a pattern I kept seeing: teams usually aren’t starved for customer info. They have sales calls, support conversations, analytics, research, opinions, and a pile of recordings somewhere. The harder problem is turning all of that into a shared view sturdy enough to make a consequential decision.

So I bring the people making the decision closer to the evidence—through customer interviews, product discovery, facilitation, positioning, prototypes, or whatever the decision requires.

Through How This Works co, I’ve completed 11 Bullseye Customer Sprints, three follow-on Listening Cycles, and seven other client projects, drawing on more than 150 customer interviews.

Here's the part I like—where the fog clears enough that someone can finally make the call and everyone can see why.
