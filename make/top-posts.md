# Make — top posts

Reference file. Use this to calibrate what's actually working with Skipper's audience — not just what reads well. Cross-reference with voice-samples.md for writing quality. This file adds performance signal. Saves are the most useful metric — they indicate the content had real utility, not just reach.

## What the data shows overall

- Peer audience (designers, design-adjacent) engages heavily but isn't the buyer
- Buyer audience (founders, operators, PMs) engages less visibly but saves more
- Posts that open with data or a cultural moment reach further than client scenes
- Posts that open with a client scene convert better (more profile clicks, more DMs)
- Saves signal utility — someone coming back to a post is stronger than a reaction

### 2. Design roles / trust made visual post

- **Impressions:** 2,662
- **Reactions:** 24
- **Comments:** 9
- **Saves:** 8
- **Reposts:** 1
- **Structure:** Data point → personal ("I've been told more than once") → principle
- **Entry point:** Current event (Lenny's Newsletter chart) — data first, then personal
- **Audience signal:** 36% Design Services, 36% Senior, 15% companies with 10,001+ employees
- **Save signal:** Eight saves is strong for a post without a direct CTA
- **Note:** Clear example of a non-client-scene entry point that performs well with buyers. Peer audience engages heavily, but saves suggest buyer-adjacent interest too. "Trust made visual" and "design is dead, long live design" are the key quotable lines.

#### Post

Open design roles are down — ask any designer who's looking for their next job — and a lot of hay is being made of this chart from Lenny's Newsletter on Tue (credited to TrueUp). The main message: PMs are more in demand than designers in early 2026.

My take? Job postings being down isn't the same as decreased value in design jobs.

I've been told more than once: "you'll make this strategy deck or these wireframes pretty, right?" And I've always nodded. But the 'pretty' is doing a lot of work there. Why things like button radius matters, why the onboarding flow needs to feel like reaching out for help, why a drop shadow on one screen and not the next makes someone pause before clicking — even if they can't say why.

That's not decoration, this is trust made visual. When things don't feel consistent page to page — template, design system, brand guide — people notice. They just can't tell you why.

Sheppard said it yesterday in Never Search Alone design office hours: companies say they care about user experience, retention, churn — then lay off the entire design team. Six months later, they're asking why customers are upset.

And Amy jumped in: it's also the research. A decade of working with different user types — understanding what a well-placed, well-sized, high-contrast button actually does — that's not something a PM picks up overnight, and it's not something AI has figured out yet.

Domain expertise > decoration. And so, design is dead, long live design!

Will companies and teams realize the "design debt" they're building up?

### 7. Korean Jindo / language barrier post

- **Impressions:** 8,855
- **Reactions:** 167
- **Comments:** 54
- **Structure:** Personal narrative with a cultural/linguistic twist → specific scene → open-ended question
- **Entry point:** Life transition/geographic marker — "Just before we moved..."
- **Audience signal:** Broad cross-industry resonance, deep emotional engagement
- **Save signal:** Low — high engagement/resonance but low tactical utility
- **Note:** Outperformed every other post in raw reach and comments by a landslide. Proves that hyper-specific, vulnerable storytelling with zero business agenda breaks through algorithmic noise. It drives massive profile visibility and humanizes Skipper, building top-of-funnel affinity.

#### Post

Just before we moved from NYC to the Bay area, we adopted a Korean jindo. The foster told us the dog seemed sweet — tho fairly reserved — but didn't really respond to verbal cues. As a Korean, I'd always wanted a jindo (진돗개 or jindo geh, geh meaning dog in Hangul). I'd done my homework, they were loyal, clean, very cat-like. What we didn't know: she was coming from near Busan, the 2nd largest city in South Korea and English may not have been her first language. My wife and kid had already spent a few hours with her by the time I got home from work — the tryout was basically over unbeknownst to me. In fact, I’d almost forgotten it was happening. And when I came in, I didn't crouch down, I didn't follow any of what you're supposed to do when meeting a dog for the first time, especially one who’s standoffish. I just walked in and said, Anyeong (안녕), jindo geh, joeun gaene (좋은 개네) — or, Hi jindo dog, good dog — out of reflex, or tiredness, or both. She came straight to me and rubbed herself against my legs. No wariness. I don't know what did it. Did I resemble the people she knew in Busan? Was it the bit of mangled Korean? Was it that I wasn't trying? Maybe all of it, maybe none of it. Her name is Ggachi (까치, it's a cutesy word for magpie) and she's been with us almost 10 years now. What’s something that you didn’t know about your current pet when you first got them?

## Patterns across top posts

### Entry points that work

- Current event or data point ("open design roles are down")
- Specific action on a specific day ("I signed up for a new networking app yesterday")
- Self-implicating confession ("I'm a serial hand raiser" or "What's one thing you're dragging your feet on?")
- Saturday or off-hours permission ("It's Saturday")
- Someone else's question or prompt ("Someone asked me yesterday")
- Major life, domestic, or geographic transitions ("Just before we moved from NYC to the Bay area" or "This morning I found my phone...")
- Authority retrospective tracking ("Over the last year, I've worked with 12 startup teams")
- Interactive micro-debates or internal consensus calls ("Pls settle an internal debate for me" or "Another question around LinkedIn best practices...")

### What saves signal

- Resource utility — people saving to return to later (The Career Resource List)
- Process transparency — people saving to replicate (The AI workflow breakdown)
- Framework breakdowns — tracking methodology adjustments (The Bullseye Customer Sprint evolution)
- Strong thematic analogies — people saving to share with someone else (The Pied Piper or the Trout)

### What peer engagement signals

- The post landed with the right people but not necessarily the buyers
- High reactions + low saves = resonance and emotional story buy-in (The Jindo post, the doll blanket)
- High comment/vote velocity + low saves = interactive engagement optimization (The LinkedIn optimization polls)
- High saves + lower reactions = utility without broad public reach

### What buyer engagement looks like

- Profile clicks after the post
- DMs referencing the specific post
- Saves from non-design audience segments (specifically on structural posts like #11)

### The gap to watch

Posts that perform best with the peer audience (designers, creatives) are not always the ones that generate BCS inbound. Track both — they're different content jobs.
